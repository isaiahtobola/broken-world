One long dive through every layer of the Sea — sunshine at the top, black water, plankton, and the posts floating in it. No waves this time. Past 11,000 metres the water is at its blackest — and that's where the ROV finally wakes up. Past 12,000, one tap on a plankton sets off the whole school.

≈ 200 m · Twilight Zone

The light starts letting go

The sun rays are already behind you — they only live at the top, so the surface feels like a place, not just a starting point. Red light went first — filtered out within metres of the surface, long gone by here; blue hangs on the longest, then it lets go too.

≈ 1,000 m · Midnight Zone

No sunlight past here

From this depth down, the only light is the light things make themselves — plankton sparks, and headlights. Try it: tap a plankton. Up here in the shallows only the one you touched answers — dive deeper and more of its neighbours join in, one after another, until past 12,000 m one tap lights the whole page.

Prop Post № 2
FAKE TEXT BOX · DEMO PROP · 1,000 M

The little dots drifting past are plankton, and they're alive — and how many answer a tap depends on how deep you are. From 0–3,000 m just the one you tapped lights up. From 3,000–6,000 m it lights first, then a second one a little after, each fading in and out. From 6,000–9,000 m three close together light in succession — 1, then 2, then 3, fading in and out. From 9,000–12,000 m the four nearest to each other go in succession — 1, then 2, then 3, then 4. And past 12,000 m, down at the floor, one tap sets off a chain reaction — every plankton on the page lights in succession, rolling through the whole school in a different pattern every single time. All of them ghostly turquoise-green, bright and see-through, like real bioluminescence, then they settle back down. Tap empty water and nothing happens — you have to actually touch one.

≈ 9,500 m · The Trench Deepens

Pressure: nearly a thousand atmospheres

Pressure climbs about one atmosphere for every ten metres down, so down here it's already near a thousand — and past it at the trench floor. The deepest-rated ROVs work at these depths on a tether, with crush-resistant syntactic foam for buoyancy, thrusters, sonar, and lights that cut a small room out of the dark. Ours carries the same kit — red light on its port side, green on starboard, like any vessel.

11,000 – 12,000 m · Below Challenger Deep

The bottom of the world

No light waits for you down here — the Sea has no glowing floor, just black water all the way down. But the ROV is awake: watch for him crossing in front of everything, on patrol in the trench. Tap him and his headlight cuts the dark — one long beam straight out of his lamp, washing across the trench, and no glow anywhere else on him.

12,000 – 13,000 m · Below Challenger Deep

One tap lights everything

Past 12,000 the rules change. Down here, tap one single plankton and the whole page answers: every plankton on the screen lights in succession, a chain reaction rolling through the school in one long smooth wave — and a different pattern every time, so no two taps ever light this water the same way twice. The deepest water answers the loudest.

♡ Like◍ CommentEnd of the dive

Fact-checked five times over: the zone boundaries on this dive are the real ones — sunlight 0–200 m, twilight 200–1,000 m, midnight 1,000–4,000 m, abyssal 4,000–6,000 m, hadal below 6,000 m — and the hadal zone really is named for Hades. Marine snow is real, red port / green starboard is the real convention, and sunlight really is gone by about 1,000 m — which is why the rays on this page give up right around there. Bioluminescent plankton really do flash when the water around them is disturbed. One honest stretch: the real Challenger Deep, the floor of the Mariana Trench, measures about 10,935 m down, where the pressure passes 1,000 atmospheres. This demo stretches that last stretch all the way to 13,000 m — past the real floor entirely — so the ROV gets his 11,000 m wake-up line, and the plankton get a floor of their own past 12,000 m where one tap lights the whole school.
